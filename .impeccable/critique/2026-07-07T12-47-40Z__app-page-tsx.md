## Overall Impression

This homepage has solid bones — good SEO, proper Next.js stack, smart localization, transparent business details in the footer. But it's wearing a costume that doesn't fit. The brand promises "nerdy, friendly, approachable one-person repair shop in East Iceland." The website delivers "generic SaaS startup template in dark mode." The gap between these two is where every design problem lives. The copy is warm and first-person ("Ég þjónusta..."), but the visual layer suffocates it.

**Single biggest opportunity:** Put Daði's face on the homepage. For a one-person business selling trust, the absence of a human is the most damaging design decision on the page.

## What's Working

1. **Footer transparency:** Full kennitala, VAT number, bank account, physical location, phone, email — this is genuinely excellent and trust-building. Best section on the page.

2. **Local service area focus:** Town-by-town listing (Neskaupstaður, Norðfjörður, etc.) is smart local SEO and tells East Icelanders "yes, I serve your town."

3. **Header `.is` domain suffix:** `<span className="font-mono text-xs text-tech-green">.is</span>` is the single best personality detail. Playful, nerdy, subtle. This needs to be amplified everywhere.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] NO HUMAN PRESENCE
**What:** Zero photos of Daði anywhere on the homepage. For a one-person repair shop where trust is the product, this is catastrophic. The brand's three words are Nerdy · Friendly · Approachable — and there is no face to attach any of them to.

**Why it matters:** Computer repair is a trust transaction. Someone hands over their broken machine to a stranger. Without a face, there's no person to trust. The copy says "Ég" (I) but the design says "faceless service provider."

**Fix:** Add Daði's photo in the hero (beside or below the logo), as a circular portrait, or in a "meet your tech" section. A photo of the workshop or storefront would also help.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable craft hero section` — full redesign with human presence as the centerpiece.

### [P1] AI TEMPLATE STRUCTURE
**What:** Three identical cards with identical DOM, identical classes, identical icon boxes, identical bullet lists, identical "read more →" links (`app/page.tsx:60-173`). Plus backdrop-blur on everything, gradient-line divider, and hero→cards→CTA pipeline.

**Why it matters:** For a business whose value proposition is "I build custom things," a template-made site undermines the core message. The three-card symmetry signals "I used an AI template" to anyone who's seen more than five startup websites.

**Fix:** Break the card symmetry. Differentiate them visually (size, color, layout). Or replace the grid with a narrative flow — show the repair process, not a feature list.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout home page` — restructure the content flow away from the card grid.

### [P1] INTER FONT
**What:** Inter loaded from Google Fonts (`app/layout.tsx:2`). Inter is the #1 AI-slop typography tell — it's the default font every AI coding tool reaches for.

**Why it matters:** Typography is the voice of a brand. Inter says "default, neutral, template." For an Icelandic, nerdy, approachable brand, the type should have character. This is a quick win with outsized impact.

**Fix:** Replace Inter with a distinctive pairing. A warm display font (e.g., a humanist serif or characterful sans) for headings, paired with a clean readable body font. Keep the mono `.is` detail.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable typeset` — full typography overhaul.

### [P2] LOW-ENERGY VISUAL DESIGN
**What:** Monotone gray palette (gray-900, gray-950, gray-800/50) with gray text (gray-400, gray-500). Every section has the same visual weight. The tech accent colors (green, cyan, purple, orange) defined in `tailwind.config.ts` are never used on the homepage.

**Why it matters:** The brand calls for "Bold over bland" and "Terminal warmth." The current design is neither bold nor warm — it's gray. A visitor scrolling through gets visual fatigue from the monotone diet.

**Fix:** Use the tech accent colors strategically. Add a hero background with personality. Introduce color-differentiated sections. Left-align key content for hierarchy. Add a photo or illustration to break up the text.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable colorize` — inject the unused accent colors strategically.

### [P2] ZERO SOCIAL PROOF
**What:** No testimonials, no reviews, no trust badges, no satisfaction guarantee, no "trusted by" logos anywhere on the homepage.

**Why it matters:** Repair services require high trust. Without social proof, there's nothing to convert a skeptical first-time visitor. The footer has business registration details (good!), but that requires scrolling to the bottom.

**Fix:** Add a testimonial or pull-quote. Add a Google Reviews badge. Add a simple satisfaction guarantee ("Ekkert föst greiningargjald" — no fixed diagnostic fee). Place trust signals after services and before the CTA.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable craft trust section` — build a social proof section.

### [P3] CONTRAST AND READABILITY
**What:** `text-gray-400` on `bg-gray-900`, `text-sm` (14px) body text throughout the service cards. While contrast technically scrapes by WCAG AA (~5.8:1), small gray text on dark backgrounds is fatiguing — especially for an audience that skews older.

**Fix:** Bump body text to `text-gray-300` or lighter. Increase to `text-base` (16px). Lighten content section backgrounds slightly.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable audit` — accessibility and contrast pass.

## Persona Red Flags

- Subtitle says "tölvuviðgerðir" but Jordan doesn't know the technical term for what's wrong. The bullet lists (SSD/HDD/RAM) are meaningless jargon.

## Minor Observations

- The logo uses `?v=3` cache-busting — has been changed three times, worth noting
- `tech-cyan`, `tech-purple`, and `tech-orange` are defined in tailwind config but **never used** on the homepage — dead tokens
- `bg-gradient-to-b from-gray-900 to-gray-950` appears twice (hero and CTA) — imperceptible gradient, weight without purpose
- WhatsApp pre-filled message is generic ("Hæ, ég vildi spyrja um tölvuþjónustu") — should include Daði's name and be more personal
- `hover:-translate-y-0.5` on bottom CTA button is inconsistent with hero CTA which lacks it
- Social media links have placeholder fallbacks (`'your-page-username'`, `#`) — broken if env vars aren't set
- Homepage is a client component (`'use client'`) but uses no client interactivity beyond i18n — unnecessary JS
- The projects lab section ("Ég smíða líka hugbúnaðarlausnir") dilutes the main repair-shop message

## Questions to Consider

- **If Daði walked into a room to fix someone's computer, would they recognize the homepage as belonging to the same person?** The site is generic and gray. Daði is a real human with a personality. Why does the site have zero personality?

- **The brand says "Show the person" and "Terminal warmth" — where exactly are those principles visible in this implementation?** The `.is` domain suffix is the only moment of personality on the entire page. Is the design serving the brand, or is the brand aspirational while the build stayed template-safe?

- **If a visitor lands here with a computer that won't turn on, what specifically tells them "this person in Neskaupstaður can help me, and he won't rip me off"?** The footer does this well, but only after 1000+ pixels of generic content. What if trust signals were first, not last?
