# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Individuals and small businesses in East Iceland (Neskaupstaður, Norðfjörður, Eskifjörður, Reyðarfjörður, Fjarðabyggð, Egilsstaðir, and surrounding areas). Many are not tech-savvy — they need someone they can trust with their computers, someone who speaks plain Icelandic, not jargon. They value personal service, local presence, and practical solutions over flashy promises.

**Context when visiting the site:** They have a computer problem, need an upgrade, or want advice on a purchase. They're probably stressed or uncertain. The site needs to immediately communicate competence and approachability.

**Job to be done:** Find a real person in their area who can fix/advise/build — not a faceless chain, not a call center.

## Product Purpose

Tölvuhvíslarinn is a one-person tech services company in East Iceland offering computer repair, custom desktop builds, and tech consulting. It also houses experimental software projects (text-to-speech, tools). The website exists to convert visitors into contacts (WhatsApp, email, phone) and to establish credibility as a skilled local expert.

Success is: a visitor lands, feels "this person knows what they're doing and I can just message them," and reaches out.

## Brand Personality

**Nerdy · Friendly · Approachable** — Someone who genuinely geeks out about computers but makes you feel welcome, not intimidated. The enthusiasm is real: this is a person who gets excited about a well-built PC, who explains what RAM does with genuine joy, who probably has a favorite terminal font. But the vibe is warm and human — like a friend from the local Makerspace who also happens to fix laptops.

The design should feel like a workshop you want to hang out in: tech tools on the walls, but also a coffee machine and someone who says "já, þetta reddast" when you explain your computer problem.

Voice: Conversational, playful, Icelandic-first. Light tech humor is welcome. No jargon without explanation. No corporate distancing. Imagine Daði explaining something over a cup of coffee — that's the tone. Terminal aesthetics and code references are on-brand (nerdy!), but always tempered with warmth (friendly!). Never cold, never intimidating, never "you wouldn't understand."

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS template** — Inter/Roboto font, purple-to-blue gradients, glassmorphism cards, hero→3-cards→CTA layouts, centered everything, muted gray text on tinted backgrounds
- **Corporate IT service chains** — stock photos of people in server rooms, bullet lists of certifications, formal "request a quote" flows
- **Over-designed agency sites** — excessive animation, scroll-jacking, trendy brutalism or neubrutalism that puts style ahead of clarity
- **Cold terminal / hacker aesthetic** — green-on-black matrix vibes, raw monospace everywhere, intimidating CLI references. Nerdy is playful and warm, not "access denied."
- **The current site's tells** — 3 identical card shape repeated, backdrop-blur everywhere, gradient-line dividers, Inter font as the only voice, no photographic or human presence

## Design Principles

1. **Small shop, big craft** — Every detail should signal that one skilled person made this. Hand-crafted feel, not template-assembled. Imperfect in the way that communicates care, not sloppiness.

2. **Show the person** — The site should feature Daði. A photo, a name, a direct line. The biggest trust signal for local tech services is knowing who you're dealing with.

3. **Terminal warmth** — Tech references (monospace accents, code-like details, subtle terminal nods) are welcome as personality, but always wrapped in warmth. A code comment that makes you smile. A "hjálp" command that actually helps. Never cold, never intimidating.

4. **Bold over bland** — When in doubt, choose the bolder option. Distinctive typography, confident spacing, unexpected color moves. This site should be memorable, not "clean and safe."

5. **Icelandic confidence** — The site is Icelandic-first. It doesn't need to look like a Silicon Valley startup. It should feel rooted in place — East Iceland, small town, real person. Icelandic text with proper characters throughout.

6. **One-path clarity** — Every page has one obvious next step. The site isn't a brochure to browse; it's a funnel to contact. Remove choices that don't lead to a message.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- Reasonable defaults: good contrast, readable type sizes, keyboard-navigable, semantic HTML
- Dark theme by default (current preference), but should not break if someone forces light mode
- `prefers-reduced-motion` respected throughout
- Icelandic text with proper characters (ð, þ, æ, ö, etc.) — no font fallback gaps
- Serves a broad age range — type shouldn't be tiny, touch targets should be generous
